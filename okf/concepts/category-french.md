The French-language proficiency category is the exception among categories: it
has no occupation list at all. To be eligible you must have French test results
showing a minimum score of 7 in all four language abilities on the Niveaux de
compétence linguistique canadiens, plus meet the requirements in that round's
instructions.

Because it is occupation-blind, this category is open to any candidate who can
reach NCLC 7 in French, regardless of what they do for a living.

It is also the largest and most frequent category by invitations issued, and its
cutoffs have often been far below general-round scores. That combination — no
occupation gate, large draws, low cutoffs — is why French is usually the highest
-leverage thing a candidate can change about their profile.

NCLC 7 in all four abilities is the same threshold that unlocks the 25 or 50
additional CRS points for French, so reaching it pays twice.

To be eligible for an invitation in a category-based round you must first meet
the minimum criteria for Express Entry, including being eligible for one of the
three programs it covers. Category eligibility is checked on top of that, never
instead of it.
