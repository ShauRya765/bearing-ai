IRCC counts work experience in hours, not calendar time. One year of
experience means 1,560 hours. Full-time is defined as 30 hours per week, so
30 hours a week for 52 weeks equals 1,560 hours — one year.

"Full-time or an equal amount in part-time" means the number of hours you
work each week does not matter, as long as the total reaches 1,560 hours for
each year of experience required. Part-time simply means fewer hours per
week, so it takes more weeks to reach the same total.

Examples that each equal one year (1,560 hours):
- 30 hours per week for 12 months (full-time)
- 20 hours per week for about 18 months (part-time)
- 15 hours per week for 24 months (part-time)

Three rules that are easy to miss:
- Hours above 30 per week do not count as extra. Working 40 hours a week does
  not reach the total any faster; the count is capped at 30 hours per week.
- You can combine more than one job. Two part-time jobs of 15 hours per week
  each add up to 30 hours per week, which counts as full-time.
- The work must be paid. Volunteer work, unpaid internships, and work done
  for study credit do not count, and the experience must be in the same type
  of job you are claiming.

To convert any part-time schedule into calendar time: months needed equals
1,560 divided by your weekly hours, divided by 4.33 (the average number of
weeks in a month).
