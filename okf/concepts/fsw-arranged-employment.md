Arranged employment is worth up to 10 of the 67 selection-factor points for the
Federal Skilled Worker Program. This is the pairing that confuses people: the
Comprehensive Ranking System stopped giving job offer points on March 25, 2025,
but the FSW eligibility grid never did.

A valid job offer for these points must be for continuous, paid, full-time work
of at least 30 hours a week, not seasonal, for at least 1 year, in a TEER 0, 1, 2
or 3 occupation. IRCC must also be satisfied that you can do the job and can
become licensed or certified in Canada if the occupation is regulated.

Beyond that, one of four situations must apply:

1. You work in Canada on a work permit issued on a positive labour market impact
   assessment, for the employer named on the permit, and that employer has made
   the offer.
2. You work in Canada in an LMIA-exempt job under an international agreement, a
   significant-benefit provision or a federal-provincial agreement, and have been
   with that employer for at least 1 year of continuous full-time or equivalent
   part-time work, and that employer has made the offer.
3. You do not hold a work permit and do not plan to work in Canada before your
   permanent resident visa, and an employer with an LMIA has made you a valid job
   offer based on it.
4. You work in Canada in an LMIA-exempt job that is exempt for some other reason,
   and a different employer, who holds an LMIA, has made you a valid job offer
   based on it.

Earning these 10 points also earns 5 adaptability points, so a qualifying offer
is worth 15 of the 67 in practice.
