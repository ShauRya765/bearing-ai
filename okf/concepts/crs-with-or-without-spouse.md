The CRS has two parallel sets of tables, and which one applies is not simply a
question of whether you are married.

You are scored using the without-a-spouse columns if:

- your spouse or common-law partner is not coming with you to Canada, or
- your spouse or common-law partner is a Canadian citizen or permanent resident.

In both cases you get the higher core maximums — 110 for age, 150 for education,
160 for language, 80 for Canadian work experience — and the spouse factors group
does not apply at all.

This is worth checking before assuming a marriage costs points. A candidate
married to a Canadian permanent resident scores exactly as a single candidate
does. A candidate whose spouse stays behind also scores as single, though that
declaration has to be true and is assessed with the application.

The comparison that matters is 40 points of extra core capacity — the difference
between a 500-point and a 460-point core maximum — against a spouse group worth
at most 40. Those cancel only if the spouse maxes all three of their factors,
which is rare, so for most couples the higher-scoring partner should be the
principal applicant.
