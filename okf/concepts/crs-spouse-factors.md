When a spouse or common-law partner is coming with you to Canada, they are
scored in their own capped group worth up to 40 points:

- Level of education — up to 10 points. Secondary school 2, one-year credential
  6, two-year 7, bachelor's 8, two-or-more credentials 9, master's or
  professional degree 10, doctorate 10.
- Official language proficiency — up to 20 points, at up to 5 per ability. CLB 4
  or less 0, CLB 5 or 6 gives 1, CLB 7 or 8 gives 3, CLB 9 or more gives 5.
- Canadian work experience — up to 10 points. 1 year 5, 2 years 7, 3 years 8,
  4 years 9, 5 years or more 10.

These 40 points do not simply add to your score. Bringing a spouse lowers your
own core maximums — age from 110 to 100, education from 150 to 140, language from
160 to 150 and Canadian work experience from 80 to 70. That is 40 points of core
capacity given up in exchange for a group worth at most 40.

At the ceiling it is a wash. In practice it is usually a loss, because the core
reduction applies in full the moment a spouse is declared as accompanying, while
the 40 points on this side are only reached by a spouse holding a doctorate, CLB
9 in all four abilities and five years of Canadian work experience.
