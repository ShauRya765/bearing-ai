Age is worth up to 110 points without a spouse or common-law partner, and up to
100 with one. The curve peaks early and decays steeply:

- 17 or younger: 0 points either way.
- 18: 99 without a spouse, 90 with.
- 19: 105 without, 95 with.
- 20 to 29: the maximum — 110 without, 100 with.
- 30: 105 without, 95 with.
- 35: 77 without, 70 with.
- 40: 50 without, 45 with.
- 44: 6 without, 5 with.
- 45 or older: 0 either way.

The drop is not linear. Between 40 and 44 a candidate without a spouse loses 44
points in four years, more than the entire second-language section is worth.

Age is the one core factor you cannot improve, which is why it usually sets the
deadline for everything else: a candidate at 29 has a reason to test and submit
before their birthday that a candidate at 25 does not.
